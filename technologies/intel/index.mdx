# Intel

Intel was formally incorporated on July 18, 1968, in Santa Clara, California, by Robert Noyce and Gordon Moore after they left Fairchild Semiconductor. Over more than five decades it grew into one of the defining companies in computing, and it now applies that silicon expertise to AI through a combination of general-purpose processors, dedicated AI accelerators, and an open-source software stack for optimizing and deploying models.

## Core Products

### Xeon and Core Ultra Processors
Intel's Xeon server CPUs and Core Ultra client processors provide the general-purpose compute backbone for AI workloads, with Xeon 6 targeting large-scale data center deployments and Core Ultra targeting on-device AI PCs.

### Gaudi AI Accelerators
Gaudi is Intel's line of purpose-built deep learning accelerators, with Gaudi 3 designed for large-scale training and inference clusters as an alternative to GPU-based systems.

### OpenVINO Toolkit
An open-source toolkit for optimizing and deploying deep learning inference across computer vision, speech recognition, generative AI, and NLP, supporting models from PyTorch, TensorFlow, ONNX, and other frameworks, and deploying from edge devices to cloud servers.

### Intel Tiber AI Cloud
A cloud platform offering access to single nodes or clusters of Gaudi AI processors, Xeon CPUs, and Max GPUs, with pre-configured environments for rapid AI proof-of-concepts and production-level workloads.

## Key Features

**Heterogeneous AI Compute**
Intel's AI stack spans CPUs (Xeon, Core Ultra), discrete GPUs (Max, Arc), and dedicated accelerators (Gaudi), letting developers match workloads to the most cost-effective hardware for training or inference.

**Open-Source Optimization Tooling**
OpenVINO includes the Neural Network Compression Framework (NNCF) for quantization and sparsity, a GenAI repository for generative AI pipelines, and OpenVINO Model Server for scalable model serving.

**Framework Interoperability**
Optimum Intel integrates OpenVINO with the Hugging Face API, and Intel has contributed GPU support directly into PyTorch, reducing the work needed to move existing models onto Intel hardware.

## Use Cases

**Edge and On-Device Inference**
Deploy computer vision, speech, and small language models on Core Ultra AI PCs and embedded Intel hardware using OpenVINO's edge-optimized runtime.

**Large-Scale Training and Inference**
Run large language model training and inference on Gaudi 3 clusters or Xeon 6 servers, either on-premises or through Intel Tiber AI Cloud.
