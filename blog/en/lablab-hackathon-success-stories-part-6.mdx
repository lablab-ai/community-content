The hardest test of an artificial intelligence system is not whether it generates a plausible answer in a clean demo. It is whether it survives contact with operational reality.

In earlier installments of this series, we documented builders who [created guardrails to stop autonomous agents from acting badly](https://lablab.ai/ai-articles/lablab-hackathon-success-stories-part-2), engineers who [tackled the unglamorous plumbing first](https://lablab.ai/ai-articles/lablab-hackathon-success-stories-part-4), and teams who [exposed systemic blind spots in AI workflows](https://lablab.ai/ai-articles/lablab-hackathon-success-stories-part-5). In this sixth chapter, five builders and teams confronted the gap between high-level model outputs and messy, ground-truth execution: corporate strategy teams losing days to manual competitor tracking, physical product founders gambling savings on unvalidated inventory, institutional real estate models leaning on backward-looking data, code deployments lacking auditable security consensus, and enterprise automation scripts shattering whenever a UI layout shifts.

They did not build conversational toys. They engineered systems that anchor AI to real-world ground truth.

## Hamza Imran: The Autonomous Radar That Collapses Days of Corporate Intel

Competitive intelligence is one of the most exhausting manual grinds in corporate strategy. Strategy leads, founders, and venture analysts spend days manually monitoring competitor pricing shifts, parsing job listings to deduce unannounced product roadmaps, and sitting through 90-minute quarterly earnings calls hoping to catch an offhand executive admission. By the time a comprehensive report is assembled, the market has already moved.

At the Bright Data AI x Web Data Hackathon (Web Data UNLOCKED), Hamza Imran set out to compress that multi-day manual investigation into an instant, autonomous radar. Competing solo as Team Solitude from Pakistan, he built OmniSight.

OmniSight coordinates an asynchronous multi-agent intelligence pipeline from two simple inputs: a competitor's name and an audio file—such as a keynote, podcast interview, or earnings call. Behind the scenes, Bright Data scrapes real-time hiring patterns and pricing signals directly from the competitor's digital footprint. Simultaneously, Speechmatics performs speaker diarization and audio transcription to extract strategic intent from spoken dialogue. Cognee maintains a persistent semantic knowledge graph across scans, while Gemini and Groq distill the disparate web signals and transcript data into an actionable disruption dashboard.

The build revealed a steep latency bottleneck. Running deep web scraping and audio diarization sequentially initially choked the pipeline, forcing users to wait up to five minutes per scan. Rather than stripping out data sources, Hamza re-architected the execution flow using Python's `asyncio.gather`, firing all web scraping, audio transcription, and semantic memory lookups in parallel. That single architectural shift slashed execution latency by 70%. To replace idle waiting with full operational transparency, he embedded a real-time terminal feed directly into the interface, letting users observe the agent's live reasoning loops, tool dispatches, and verification steps as they occur.

OmniSight earned 2nd Place in the Speechmatics Partner Challenge at Web Data UNLOCKED. Hamza is now preparing to evolve the prototype into a production intelligence platform for enterprise strategy departments and investment committees who need verifiable competitive insight without the manual grind.

His advice for hackathon participants: "Build alone if you like moving fast without syncing decisions with anyone. Focus on an actual operational bottleneck, find where the latency lives, and build something that compresses days of manual grind into seconds of clear insight."

## Daffa Farras Putra Tarigan & Team Singkong: Validating Consumer Demand Before Spending a Dollar

For founders launching physical consumer products, the riskiest decision happens before manufacturing ever begins. Founders routinely commit thousands of dollars to minimum order quantities, factory tooling, and upfront advertising based on little more than personal conviction, only to discover that market demand is non-existent. Traditional market research is either prohibitively expensive or months behind live consumer sentiment.

At the Web Data UNLOCKED Hackathon, Daffa Farras Putra Tarigan and Team Singkong built ConsumerIQ to eliminate that gamble.

ConsumerIQ serves as a pre-launch validation engine that measures genuine market appetite before capital is committed. A founder inputs a product concept, category, and target geography. The platform immediately deploys Bright Data's SERP and Web Scraper APIs across Amazon, Walmart, Etsy, and social media channels, aggregating real-time competitor listings, pricing floors, review sentiment, and recurring buyer complaints.

The ingested data streams through a hybrid multi-model pipeline: Llama 3.2 3B manages rapid ReAct agent loops, Qwen 3.5 handles data normalization and multilingual preprocessing, and distilled reasoning models synthesize market signals against a PostgreSQL database powered by `pgvector` and a Cognee knowledge graph. Rather than returning a generic summary, ConsumerIQ produces an empirical verdict: Build, Pivot, Stop, or Refine.

The team—comprising Daffa Tarigan, Louis Enggar Theo Dharma, Paulus Billiardo Putrakedua, Mohammad Ezzeddin Pratama, and Keisha Putri Theanny—won 1st Place in the Featherless AI Track and placed as a global finalist. ConsumerIQ proves that empirical web data can protect early-stage founders from costly inventory mistakes before the first unit is produced.

## Laurie Sartain: When Real-World Domain Expertise Defines the Ceiling

Laurie Sartain brings a perspective rare among hackathon participants. With thirteen years in institutional real estate investment management—including advising on $116 billion in debt at CIRM, managing a $2.5 billion levered portfolio at PCCP, and holding a mathematics degree from the University of Chicago—she serves as one of Anthropic's first Claude Ambassadors for Real Estate. She builds software because the analytical tools the industry requires simply did not exist.

Across two consecutive hackathons, Laurie demonstrated how deep domain fluency transforms abstract AI primitives into predictive systems.

At the Agentic Economy on Arc Hackathon in San Francisco, she built Gyasss as a solo developer, taking 2nd Place. In high-value, non-public markets, critical data has historically moved through private, informal networks with no mechanism to verify and exchange information at the exact moment it holds value. Gyasss tested that dynamic as a transaction-verified pricing oracle built on Circle Nanopayments: everyday contributors earn USDC for verifying localized pricing data, while autonomous AI agents pay $0.001 per query to consume the verified stream.

Two months later at Web Data UNLOCKED, Laurie teamed up with Salim Masmoudi to build HomeStar, winning 1st Place in the Finance Track. When institutional investors forecast rent growth, most models rely on trailing twelve-month lease data, supply projections, and broad macroeconomic statistics. HomeStar flips the predictive timeline by tracking labor dynamics instead. Using Bright Data to monitor job postings, quit rates, and hiring velocity, the system captures migration signals months before leases are signed, recognizing that people move for employment long before signing a lease. In walk-forward backtests across held-out metropolitan areas, HomeStar consistently outperformed fitted baseline models out of sample.

Laurie's takeaway for builders cuts through technical hype: "Plenty of people can build. Fewer can say which problem is worth solving, or what correct looks like when the model hands you an answer. Domain expertise is indispensable, and product sense is the ceiling."

## Sinthanavanh Sinsamphanh & Team Impactonious: The Multi-Agent PR Security Gate That Refused to Be a Trench Coat

Sinthanavanh Sinsamphanh (Dan) entered the Band of Agents Hackathon with no prior hackathon experience. A 19-year-old first-year business student at the National University of Laos who works on Lao-language speech models in Vientiane, Dan wanted to test his execution speed against an international deadline.

His breakthrough began by scrapping his original concept on day one. He had initially designed an onboarding assistant with five agents summarizing repositories for new developers. But as he examined the architecture, he realized the agents had no genuine need for one another—they were simply five workers reporting to one central synthesizer. As Dan described it, it was "five API calls in a trench coat."

He discarded the project and asked a harder question: *What breaks if you remove the multi-agent platform?* If the answer was nothing, the project was hollow.

DeployGuard was born from that discipline. Built with teammates Thavisouk Douangphachanh and Phetthavee Bounlavong, DeployGuard transforms pull request reviews into an autonomous, auditable security team. Triggered by GitHub webhooks, a Band collaboration room activates where five specialist agents coordinate via @mentions: ScanAgent runs linting and dependency checks, SecurityAgent executes vulnerability and secret detection, RiskAgent calculates a weighted risk score, DeployAgent executes deployment only upon clearance, and ReportAgent logs the complete audit trail.

Two architectural choices set DeployGuard apart. First, every critical security decision is governed by deterministic code rather than probabilistic model judgment, ensuring consistent evaluations even when utilizing lightweight open-source models. Second, the pipeline fails closed: any unverified change halts the flow and escalates for human intervention. RiskAgent also independently audits SecurityAgent's findings, forcing a consensus cross-check before code can reach production.

DeployGuard earned Finalist honors and took 2nd Place in the Featherless Track. Dan's advice for first-time builders: "Ask what breaks if you remove the platform you're building on. If the answer is 'not much,' change the idea while it's still cheap to change. And if you're nineteen and unsure whether you belong in the room—enter anyway."

## Jhon Anthoric Concepcion & Team BISAKOLAI: Vision-Native Automation That Navigates Software Like a Human

Traditional Robotic Process Automation (RPA) is notoriously brittle. The instant an enterprise application updates its user interface, alters a CSS selector, or modifies an underlying DOM structure, traditional automation scripts break. Maintaining enterprise bots becomes an expensive, ongoing engineering tax.

At the AMD Developer Hackathon, Jhon Anthoric Concepcion and Team BISAKOLAI set out to build an automation platform that does not rely on fragile code selectors.

Automato is a vision-native RPA platform designed to interact with software the same way human operators do: through sight. Rather than scraping HTML trees or relying on brittle APIs, Automato visually scans desktop applications and issues physical OS mouse clicks and keystrokes via `pygetui`. If an interface element is visible to a human eye, Automato can interact with it.

The platform combines Qwen 3.6 for multimodal vision-language reasoning, a Pinecone vector database for context persistence, and grounded Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) running on AMD hardware. By allowing non-technical operators—such as accountants, office administrators, and operations leads—to construct resilient cross-platform workflows using natural English instructions, Automato eliminates the dependency on developer maintenance.

The build secured Overall 3rd Best Solution at the AMD Developer Hackathon. Team BISAKOLAI—including Jhon Concepcion, Serge Jossiah Calasara, Casimir Elijah Bajarla, Rex Peruelo, and Steve Tacay—proved that visual grounding makes automation resilient to interface changes that routinely break legacy scripts.

## What These Five Share

None of these builders treated artificial intelligence as a magic wand. 

Hamza Imran refused to accept a five-minute latency penalty, using asynchronous concurrency to build an instant competitive radar. Daffa Tarigan's team replaced founder intuition with empirical e-commerce signals before capital was wasted on physical inventory. Laurie Sartain proved that thirteen years of institutional real estate experience outperforms naive models every time. Dan Sinsamphanh threw away an easy concept to build a fail-closed multi-agent security gate with real inter-agent accountability. And Jhon Concepcion's team recognized that teaching AI to see applications is far more durable than forcing it to parse fragile code.

Building under hackathon deadlines strips away superficial polish and exposes whether an architecture actually works. When software is grounded in operational reality, the impact endures long after the competition concludes.
